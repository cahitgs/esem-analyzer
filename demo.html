A three-factor worked example

Every model in this guide ships with its real Mplus 8.3 output. Nothing to install; your files never leave the page.

A synthetic 15-item dataset mirrors the 3-factor / bifactor structure of Swami, Maïano & Morin (2023). Follow the same four steps the app uses, Data → Model → Syntax → Results, and open any step with the example preloaded: CFA, ESEM, bifactor, invariance across groups and across time, and ESEM-within-CFA.

The workflow, step by step

1The data

data.dat has 16 columns: items i1–i15 and gender. When you load your own file, the app detects the delimiter, a header row and decimal commas, and can write a clean Mplus-ready .dat from a semicolon CSV.

Download data.dat Open the Data step

2Sketch the model

On the Λ target-pattern matrix set three factors (i1–i5 → F1, i6–i10 → F2, i11–i15 → F3), pick a rotation (Geomin or Target), the estimator (MLR), and the models to generate. The manuscript on the right draws the hypothesized model as Figure 1 while you click; the syntax preview opens on the Mplus sheet.

The Model step with the three-factor pattern preloaded.

Open the Model step (3 factors preloaded)

3Generate the syntax, run it

The app writes exact Mplus .inp for every model: CFA, ESEM (Geomin and Target), bifactor-CFA, bifactor-ESEM and the six invariance steps, wrapped under the 90-character limit. Click a file to read it on the Mplus sheet. Started on your own computer (node serve.mjs, or the desktop .exe from the releases page), the step finds your Mplus, shows its version in the topbar, and Run all in Mplus runs the models one after another; each output joins the manuscript as it finishes. Online, download the files or use Auto mode (Chrome/Edge): a local folder with a RUN-ALL.bat that imports the results as Mplus writes them. The optional Convergence aids card adds Morin's remedies for stubborn models.

The Syntax step on a computer with Mplus: two models run and written into the manuscript.

Open the Syntax step

4Read it as APA

Each .out becomes a row of the fit table and a Morin-style loadings table (Hoyle Handbook, Table 2): items grouped into subscale blocks with a per-block ω row, target loadings bold, non-significant loadings italic, plus auto-written prose and an interactive path diagram. Several outputs share one fit-comparison table. Copy for Word and .docx export the very same tables in the same layout. For this demo the runs are already done: every output in example-dataset/ is a real Mplus 8.3 result.

Five measurement models compared in one fit table.

5Bifactor diagram

The diagrams follow the article's figures and are interactive: toggle cross-loadings and uniquenesses, show or hide factors, drag the ellipses, export SVG or PNG. Colour carries meaning: amber ellipses are the specific factors, the navy ellipse is the general factor reaching every item.

Bifactor-ESEM path diagram: three specific factors and the general factor.

Open the bifactor-ESEM result

6Invariance across groups

The six inv_*.out files (men vs women) collapse into one comparison table: configural → metric → scalar → strict → variance–covariance → latent means, with Satorra–Bentler scaled Δχ², ΔCFI, ΔRMSEA and a supported / not supported verdict (Chen, 2007).

Tests of measurement invariance across two groups.

Open the invariance sequence

7Invariance across time

The same six steps also run longitudinally: one single-group model per step, each wave as its own ESEM set, loadings equated across waves, every indicator's residual correlated with itself over time. Sequences are generated for CFA, ESEM and bifactor-ESEM. This demo drops twelve outputs from a 2-wave well-being example: invariance holds through variance–covariance, and the latent-means step flags the built-in growth, which the bifactor sequence places on the general factor only.

Longitudinal invariance tables for the ESEM and bifactor-ESEM sequences.

Open the longitudinal sequences

8Convert to ESEM-within-CFA

Because the ESEM output carries a SVALUES block, Results shows an ESEM-within-CFA card: it picks a referent indicator per factor, fixes that referent's cross-loadings to their ESEM values, and writes a runnable CFA you can embed in larger SEM, MIMIC or DIF models (Morin's referent method). Verified in Mplus to reproduce the source ESEM exactly (df 63, log-likelihood −11107.13 in both). On a computer with Mplus the card runs the new model from here too.
